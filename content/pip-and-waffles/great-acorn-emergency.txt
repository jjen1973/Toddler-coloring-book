# **Pip and Waffles and the Great Acorn Emergency**

Pip the little blue bird woke up with a very important feeling.

Something was wrong.

She looked left.

She looked right.

She looked under her wing.

Nothing.

Then she heard it.

**BONK!**

An acorn dropped out of the tree and landed directly on Waffles the white rabbit’s head.

“Ow.”

Pip blinked.

Waffles looked up.

Another acorn fell.

**BONK!**

“Ow!”

Pip tried very hard not to laugh.

She failed.

“PIP!”

“I’m sorry!”

“You are laughing at my injuries.”

“It was a very small injury.”

Another acorn fell.

Waffles jumped sideways.

“HA! Missed me!”

**BONK!**

A second acorn bounced off his bottom.

Pip fell off the branch laughing.

Waffles stared at the tree.

“This tree has declared war.”

“It’s just dropping acorns.”

“Exactly what an evil tree would WANT you to think.”

Pip fluttered down beside him.

Waffles picked up an acorn.

He sniffed it.

“Can I eat this?”

“No.”

Waffles put it down.

“Worst tree ever.”

Then Pip noticed something strange.

Acorns were everywhere.

On the path.

Under the bushes.

Beside the log.

There were acorns behind Waffles.

There were acorns in front of Waffles.

There was even—

Pip gasped.

“Waffles.”

“What?”

“There’s one on your head.”

Waffles froze.

“Get it off.”

Pip stared.

“GET IT OFF!”

Pip pecked it away.

Waffles shook himself.

“We have an emergency.”

Pip tilted her head.

“What kind of emergency?”

“An acorn emergency.”

“That isn’t a thing.”

“It is now.”

Waffles hopped toward the biggest pile.

“We need to move them.”

“Why?”

“So nobody gets bonked.”

Pip thought about this.

Another acorn fell.

**BONK!**

Waffles rubbed his head.

Pip nodded.

“Fair point.”

They started collecting acorns.

Pip carried one in her beak.

Waffles carried three in his mouth.

“Mmmph mmmph mmmph.”

“What?”

“Mmmph!”

“Waffles, I can’t understand you.”

Waffles spit the acorns out.

“I SAID I NEED A BASKET.”

“We don’t have a basket.”

“Then we need a bucket.”

“We don’t have a bucket.”

“A wagon?”

“No wagon.”

“A wheelbarrow?”

“No.”

“A large goose?”

Pip paused.

“What would we do with a goose?”

“I hadn’t gotten that far.”

They decided to use a big leaf.

Waffles piled acorns onto it.

Pip pulled from the front.

Waffles pushed from behind.

“Ready?” asked Pip.

“Ready!”

They pulled.

Nothing happened.

Waffles pushed harder.

Pip pulled harder.

The leaf moved one tiny inch.

“Again!”

They pulled.

They pushed.

They grunted.

They groaned.

Then—

**RIIIIIP!**

The leaf tore in half.

Acorns flew everywhere.

One landed in a puddle.

**PLOP!**

One landed in a bush.

**POOF!**

One landed right between Waffles’ ears.

Pip looked at him.

Waffles looked at Pip.

“Do not laugh.”

Pip puffed out her cheeks.

“Pip.”

She squeaked.

“PIP.”

Pip exploded with laughter.

Waffles started laughing too.

Then they heard a tiny voice.

“Excuse me.”

Pip stopped laughing.

Waffles looked around.

“Did the tree just talk?”

“No.”

“Excuse me!”

A squirrel popped out from behind the log.

He stared at the scattered acorns.

His mouth fell open.

“My acorns!”

Waffles pointed at himself.

“Your acorns?”

“Yes!”

Pip looked at the giant pile.

“All of them?”

The squirrel nodded.

“I’ve been gathering them for winter.”

Waffles slowly looked at Pip.

Pip slowly looked at Waffles.

Waffles whispered, “We may have made the emergency worse.”

The squirrel looked horrified.

“My beautiful pile!”

Pip flew into action.

“We can fix it!”

Waffles nodded.

“Yes! We are excellent fixers.”

Pip stared at the ripped leaf.

Then at the acorns in the puddle.

Then at the acorn still balanced between Waffles’ ears.

“We are... acceptable fixers.”

For the rest of the afternoon, the three friends gathered every acorn.

Pip found them from above.

The squirrel carried them in his cheeks.

Waffles pushed them with his nose.

At one point, Waffles tried carrying eight at once.

He made it exactly two hops.

Then—

**WHOOSH!**

Acorns rolled in every direction.

The squirrel sighed.

Pip sighed.

Waffles smiled.

“I almost had it.”

Finally, the pile was rebuilt.

The squirrel beamed.

“Perfect!”

Pip smiled.

Waffles rubbed his belly.

“Great. Now that we saved the acorns, is anyone hungry?”

The squirrel stared at him.

Pip stared at him.

“What?” said Waffles.

“You are always hungry,” said Pip.

“I’m growing.”

“You’re a full-grown rabbit.”

“I’m growing emotionally.”

Pip laughed.

The squirrel laughed.

Waffles laughed too.

Then—

**BONK!**

Another acorn landed on Waffles’ head.

Waffles looked up at the tree.

“That’s it.”

He pointed one paw dramatically toward the branches.

“You and me, tree. Tomorrow.”

Pip shook her head.

“Come on, Waffles.”

“Where are we going?”

“To find dinner.”

Waffles’ ears popped up.

“Strawberries?”

“Maybe.”

“Clover?”

“Probably.”

“Mushrooms?”

“No.”

“Suspicious berries?”

“Absolutely not.”

Waffles grinned.

“Perfect.”

And off they went—

one little blue bird,

one very hungry white rabbit,

one relieved squirrel,

and one tree that Waffles was still keeping a very close eye on.

**The End**
